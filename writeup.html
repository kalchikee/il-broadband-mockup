Part 1 — Persona & Scenario of Use

User Persona

Maria Thornton
Senior Broadband Policy Coordinator — Illinois Office of Broadband, DCEO
Background 10 years in state economic development and public policy. Master's in Public Administration.
Technical Comfort Comfortable with Google Maps, ArcGIS Online dashboards, and Excel. Not a GIS specialist.
Primary Goal Identify and rank Illinois census tracts for BEAD federal broadband funding applications.
Pain Points Raw FCC coverage data is hard to interpret. Needs a tool that combines equity metrics, not just infrastructure coverage.

Scenario of Use

It is a Tuesday morning, three weeks before the BEAD program application deadline. Maria opens her laptop to a browser bookmark labeled "IL Broadband Tool." She needs to justify prioritizing five counties for infrastructure funding in front of her director and federal partners — and she needs data, not just intuition.

The tool loads a choropleth map of Illinois, shaded by the Broadband Need Index. The darkest red-orange areas cluster visibly in southern and western Illinois — exactly where Maria has been hearing complaints from county commissioners. She notices Alexander County near Cairo standing out immediately. She clicks "How is this calculated?" and reads the formula: 40% coverage gap, 30% income gap, 30% no-internet rate. This makes sense to her — it reflects both infrastructure deficits and socioeconomic vulnerability. She trusts the score.

She clicks on a tract in Alexander County. The popup shows: Need Index 74 (High), broadband coverage 61%, median household income $27,800, 22% without any internet subscription. This is exactly the data point she needs for her grant narrative. She switches the layer to "Coverage Percentage" to visually separate the infrastructure gap from the demographic gap — the map shifts, revealing that some tracts in Cook County have better coverage but still show up orange on the Need Index because of low income. The index is doing its job.

Maria uses the County Filter dropdown to isolate Pulaski County and compare it to Champaign County. She sees immediately in the statewide charts that rural tracts in Pulaski have far lower coverage than university-town Champaign. She screenshots both views and pastes them into her PowerPoint. By 10:00 AM — before her director's meeting — she has a ranked list of five counties with defensible, data-backed justifications. The tool gave her 40 minutes of productive analysis instead of an afternoon of spreadsheet wrestling.

Why this persona matters for design: Maria is not a GIS expert — she needs the map to communicate immediately without explanation. The Need Index must feel trustworthy and explainable. She will not use the tool for academic exploration; she needs to reach a decision quickly. Every design choice (color scale, popup layout, methodology toggle, charts) is oriented toward helping her communicate need, not just see it.

Part 2 — Mockup Summary

The mockup for this project is a working beta prototype — a fully functional web application that demonstrates the intended look, feel, and core interactions of the final Illinois Broadband Gap Analysis Tool. The prototype is built with Leaflet.js (interactive mapping), Chart.js (data visualization), and vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript, requiring no external services or logins to run.

How to Access the Prototype

  1. Navigate to the Assignments/Mockup/ folder in the project files.
  2. Double-click serve.bat — a command window will open and start a local web server.
  3. Open your browser and go to:
    http://localhost:8001
  4. The map will load with all 3,263 Illinois census tracts shaded by Broadband Need Index.
  5. Keep the command window open while using the tool. Press Ctrl+C to stop the server when done.

What the Prototype Shows

  • 🗺️Interactive Choropleth Map — Illinois census tracts colored by four data layers: Broadband Need Index, Coverage %, Median Income, and No Internet Access.
  • 💬Click Popups — Clicking any tract shows a popup with the tract's key stats: need score, coverage rate, income, population, and no-internet rate.
  • 🔢Need Index Methodology Toggle — A "How is this calculated?" button below the Broadband Need Index layer option expands to show the three weighted components.
  • 📍County Filter — A dropdown zooms the map to any of Illinois' 102 counties and highlights its tracts.
  • 📊Summary Dashboard — Right panel shows statewide counts (total tracts, high-need tracts, underserved population, avg. coverage) and a donut chart of tracts by need level.
  • ⚡Context Panel — "Why Broadband Access Matters" explains the real-world stakes for students, workers, healthcare, and federal funding.

What Is Not Yet in This Beta

The bottom of the left panel notes planned features not yet implemented in this prototype: range sliders to filter tracts by coverage threshold, income ceiling, and minimum need score — as well as a CSV data export and print view. These do not affect the core look and feel being tested.

What Feedback We Are Looking For

Peer reviewers should focus on: (1) Is the Broadband Need Index intuitively communicated by the color map? (2) Does the methodology explanation build trust in the score? (3) Is the layout easy to navigate for a non-GIS audience? (4) Does the need-level donut chart and "Why Broadband Matters" panel add useful context?

Part 3 — User Testing Plan

Please complete the following tasks as if you are a state broadband policy analyst trying to identify high-need areas for infrastructure investment. Work through each task and answer the questions that follow. There are no right or wrong answers — your honest reactions are the most useful feedback.

Setup (1 minute)

Run serve.bat and open the prototype URL above. Wait for the map to fully load — you will see Illinois colored in shades of green to red. You are now ready to begin.

1
First Impressions — Broadband Need Index Map
Look at the map without clicking anything for 30 seconds. Do not read any panel text yet.
  • Q1 What story does the color map tell you? Describe what geographic pattern you notice in your own words.
  • Q2 Before reading the legend, what do you assume the red/dark areas represent?
  • Q3 On a scale of 1–5, how clear is the map's message on first glance? (1 = very unclear, 5 = immediately obvious)
2
Understanding the Need Index
In the left panel, find the "Broadband Need Index" layer option. Below it, click the small button labeled "How is this calculated?"
  • Q4 Does the formula make sense to you? Which component (coverage gap, income gap, no-internet rate) surprised you most?
  • Q5 After reading the formula, does your confidence in the score go up, down, or stay the same? Why?
  • Q6 Is anything about the formula explanation unclear or missing?
3
Exploring an Individual Tract
Click on any dark-colored census tract in southern Illinois (below Springfield). Read the popup that appears.
  • Q7 Does the popup give you the information you would need to justify this area as a funding priority? What is missing, if anything?
  • Q8 Is the popup layout easy to read, or does it feel cluttered?
  • Q9 The need score is shown as a colored badge (red/orange/green). Is this color-coding intuitive?
4
Comparing Map Layers
In the left panel, switch the data layer from "Broadband Need Index" to "Coverage Percentage." Then switch to "Median Income." Observe how the map changes.
  • Q10 Does switching layers tell a noticeably different geographic story? Can you describe how the patterns change?
  • Q11 Are the four layer options clearly labeled? Is there any layer name you found confusing?
  • Q12 Is there a data layer you expected to find that is missing?
5
Filtering by County
Use the "County Filter" dropdown in the left panel to select "Alexander." The map should zoom in and highlight only Alexander County's tracts. Then switch back to "All Counties."
  • Q13 Did filtering by county behave as you expected? Was the interaction clear?
  • Q14 After filtering, do the summary stats and charts on the right update? (Note: In this beta, they do not update — they remain statewide. Does that feel like a problem?)
6
Right Panel — Charts & Context
Look at the right panel: the four summary stat cards, the bar chart (Coverage by Area Type), the donut chart (Tracts by Need Level), and the "Why Broadband Access Matters" section.
  • Q15 Do the charts help you understand the statewide situation, or do they feel redundant with the map?
  • Q16 Does the "Why Broadband Access Matters" panel add useful context, or does it feel out of place in an analysis tool?
  • Q17 Which statistic in the summary cards is most useful to you? Which is least useful?

General Feedback Questions

  • A Who do you think this tool is designed for? Does the interface feel appropriate for that audience?
  • B On a scale of 1–5, how easy was the tool to navigate overall? What was the single most confusing moment?
  • C If you had to make a funding recommendation to your director right now based only on what this tool shows, could you do it? What additional data or features would help?
  • D What is the single most important change you would suggest for the final version?
  • E Any other comments, observations, or suggestions?
